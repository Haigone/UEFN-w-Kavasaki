# GAME DESIGN BIBLE
## THE MOTEL: ROOM 13

## 1. High concept
A first-person cooperative survival-horror experience for 1–4 players, set in an isolated American roadside motel in 1986. Four friends seek shelter after their car breaks down during a storm. The motel is officially closed, but its neon sign illuminates every night at 02:13. A key to a nonexistent thirteenth room is waiting at reception.

**Player fantasy:** investigate an impossible place, understand its history, survive a relentless presence, rescue friends and decide whether to escape, expose the truth or become part of the motel.

## 2. Product pillars
1. Atmosphere before combat: fear comes from anticipation, sound, silhouettes and uncertainty.
2. Readable exploration: clues are environmental and discoverable; mandatory progress is never hidden behind arbitrary pixel hunting.
3. Fair pursuit: THE GUEST is dangerous but follows understandable rules.
4. Cooperative vulnerability: a captured player needs help, while solo players have a self-rescue route.
5. A coherent mystery: every document, room and photograph reinforces the owner / THE GUEST connection.
6. Replayable resolution: three endings with explicit requirements.

## 3. Setting and backstory
Raven Motel, an isolated roadside stop in the United States, 1986. The motel has twelve ordinary rooms, a reception, an owner's office, a service wing and an upper balcony. It has been officially closed, yet its power and telephone behave as if it is still operating.

The owner maintained a private room that does not appear on the public floor plan. Guests who entered ROOM 13 vanished or were absorbed into the motel's repeating night. The owner and THE GUEST are the same person, or two manifestations of the same identity. The reveal occurs inside ROOM 13 through a matching portrait, records and the creature's appearance.

The motel's repeating moment is 02:13. Use the time as a motif: clock faces, phone logs, register entries, neon flicker and the final radio message.

## 4. Opening sequence
- Establish rain, broken car and isolated road.
- The RAVEN MOTEL neon sign flickers on.
- Show a tall silhouette on the upper balcony briefly; it should not become an immediate combat encounter.
- Players enter reception.
- The guest register and key rack establish the motel's twelve-room public layout.
- A ROOM 13 key is present despite no such room existing.
- The phone rings and plays: “Welcome. We've been expecting you.”
- Start the first investigation objective.

## 5. Main cast
The playable group are four friends on a road trip. Keep their backstories light and mostly environmental; players may use any Fortnite avatar. Do not depend on custom character-specific voice acting for comprehension. THE GUEST is the central antagonist and the motel owner's uncanny double.

## 6. Six-act pacing
| Act | Target | Primary beats |
|---|---:|---|
| I — THE ARRIVAL | 0–8 min | Car, storm, neon, silhouette, reception, impossible key |
| II — THE REGISTER | 8–17 min | Guest register, office, rooms 01–06, missing guests |
| III — THE POWER | 17–27 min | Three fuses, electrical system, first full chase |
| IV — THE SECOND FLOOR | 27–36 min | Upper rooms, diary, child's room, hidden panel |
| V — ROOM 13 | 36–43 min | Four-photo sequence, owner reveal, service key |
| VI — NO VACANCY | 43–50 min | Final escape, branch conditions, ending |

Timing is a pacing target, not a hard timer. Provide optional environmental lore without making it mandatory.

## 7. Spaces and content

### Exterior / parking
- Broken vehicle belonging to the group.
- Wet asphalt, puddles, weeds, old signage and storm drainage.
- Neon RAVEN MOTEL sign visible from the road.
- Central courtyard with a controlled entrance.
- Dumpster / service clutter in a non-critical corner.
- One or more sightline moments to the upper balcony.
- The vehicle is revisited during the final act.

### Reception
- Dark timber counter, bell, guest register, telephone, key rack, wall clock, CRT/security monitor and waiting furniture.
- Register puzzle: three dates or identifiers imply a three-part combination.
- The ROOM 13 key is visible and deliberately anomalous.
- Office access is gated by the reception puzzle.

### Owner's office
- Desk, filing cabinet, photographs, old calendar, employee records, keys and a wall of motel imagery.
- A photo puzzle gives access to the upper floor.
- Evidence foreshadows the owner's identity.
- Do not reveal the full twist too early.

### Storage and electrical room
- Shelves, maintenance supplies, fuse cabinet, breaker panel, wet floor warnings and service tools.
- Three fuses are found in distinct spaces and installed in a clear order or clearly labelled sockets.
- Power restoration changes lighting and enables later progression.
- The electrical interaction creates an audible risk that can attract THE GUEST.

### Ground-floor rooms
- **01:** CRT displays the corridor from an impossible camera angle. The image can foreshadow a creature location or hidden clue.
- **02:** opened suitcase, personal effects and one flashlight battery.
- **03:** cracked bathroom mirror; THE GUEST appears in reflection but is not necessarily physically present.
- **04:** telephone and a short clue recording.
- **05:** empty room; footsteps sound above the ceiling despite no room being there.
- **06:** photographs of missing guests and a link to the ROOM 13 mystery.

### Upper floor
- Six visible rooms 07–12, accessible from the balcony corridor.
- **09:** diary, primary history evidence.
- **11:** child's room, toys and an unsettling drawing.
- **12:** sealed wall / concealed panel that leads to the secret passage.
- Remaining rooms support atmosphere, clues and hiding opportunities without redundant mandatory puzzles.

### ROOM 13
An impossible room that does not fit the visible building footprint. Include a bed, wardrobe, CRT, telephone, old photographs, owner portrait and a clock frozen at 02:13. The room should feel spatially wrong without making navigation unfair.

Required puzzle: arrange four photographs chronologically. The correct order reveals the owner's identity / opens the hidden compartment. The room contains proof that the owner is identical to THE GUEST. The reward is the Service Key or the route to it.

### Final service route
- Connect ROOM 13 to the service side of the motel.
- Route players to the generator/gate or vehicle.
- The final chase must have at least one loop or alternate path and no unavoidable dead end.
- Car keys and service access are used to complete the escape branch.

## 8. Puzzle design sheets

### Puzzle A — Guest register
**Purpose:** open reception desk drawer / obtain Office Key.
- Clues: three guest entries, dates and a repeated anomaly.
- Player action: inspect entries, infer a three-part code, enter at a desk lock.
- Success: distinct mechanical click, drawer opens, Office Key available.
- Failure: restrained feedback; no permanent lockout.
- Solo: all clues can be read and entered by one player.
- Co-op: any player may enter; completion is team-shared.

### Puzzle B — Owner's photographs
**Purpose:** open access to upper floor.
- Clues: photographs with identifiable events/years and one hidden visual match.
- Player action: align, select or order photographs.
- Success: mechanism unlocks; upper-floor key/access becomes available.
- Failure: resettable.
- Solo and co-op use same solution.

### Puzzle C — Electrical restoration
**Purpose:** restore lights and service systems.
- Find Fuse A, Fuse B and Fuse C in the motel.
- Insert each into a clearly labelled slot; if a sequence is needed, clue it in the room.
- Correct installation restores power and updates objective state.
- Wrong installation produces safe feedback and does not consume an irreplaceable fuse.
- The activation sound may trigger investigation.

### Puzzle D — ROOM 13 photographs
**Purpose:** reveal the truth and unlock service access.
- Four photographs correspond to four events in the owner's timeline.
- Order them chronologically using dates and visual clues.
- Success: hidden compartment opens; owner reveal and Service Key access.
- Failure: resettable and no item loss.
- Co-op: one player may operate while others inspect; state syncs to team.

### Puzzle E — Final escape
**Purpose:** finish the experience.
- Obtain Car Keys and Service Key through story progression.
- Restore gate/generator or service mechanism.
- Activate vehicle and complete final chase.
- Ending branch is evaluated from explicit flags, not timing races.

## 9. Narrative text and voice lines
All in-game content is English.

Known mandatory lines:
- Phone at reception: “Welcome. We've been expecting you.”
- Escape ending radio: “Thank you for staying at the Raven Motel. We hope to see you again soon.”

Suggested short environmental fragments:
- Register stamp: “NO CHECK-OUT RECORDED.”
- Office note: “If the sign is lit, do not answer the desk phone.”
- Electrical label: “MAIN POWER — STAFF ONLY.”
- Diary fragment: “The room was never built. It was always here.”
- Child's drawing caption: “HE WAITS OUTSIDE THE DOOR.”
- ROOM 13 clock label: “02:13”.
These are proposed authored lines and should be reviewed for consistency before implementation.

Keep notes concise and legible in first-person view. Provide subtitles for voice and radio. No required clue should rely only on audio.

## 10. Ending definitions

### Ending 1 — ESCAPE
Condition: players complete core escape prerequisites and choose/trigger the vehicle escape without completing the optional full truth chain.
Sequence: players reach the car, escape through the gate in a chase. Radio plays: “Thank you for staying at the Raven Motel. We hope to see you again soon.” A silhouette appears in the rear seat or rear-view reflection. Cut to black.

### Ending 2 — THE TRUTH
Condition: complete the core progression plus the full evidence chain and ROOM 13 revelation; then use the service route to confront the source or expose the truth.
Sequence: reveal the owner's identity as THE GUEST. Resolve the mystery with a distinct final cinematic and an ending-specific title card. The ending should feel like a costly but meaningful confrontation, not a generic alternate escape.

### Ending 3 — THE NEW GUEST
Condition: explicit choice to accept the motel's invitation, or a carefully authored failure/cycle branch. Avoid triggering this ending accidentally from an ordinary capture.
Sequence: the motel resets; one player becomes the next keeper/guest and the neon sign lights at 02:13. In co-op, define a fair team-wide result (e.g. group choice/vote or shared cycle) rather than randomly selecting a player without consent.

## 11. Emotional and visual pacing
- Arrival: uncertainty, distance, storm and a brief silhouette.
- Register: curiosity and small anomalies.
- Power: vulnerability and first systemic threat.
- Second floor: personal history and spatial unease.
- ROOM 13: recognition, revelation and compression of space.
- Escape: high tempo, clear navigation and final uncertainty.

Do not overuse jump scares. Build tension through sound, negative space, sightline control, repeating motifs and fair threat cues.

# CONSOLIDATED DESIGN ADDENDUM — CHAT 01

**Status:** Canon consolidation. Existing approved identity and beats above remain authoritative.

## Canonical product contract
THE MOTEL: ROOM 13; Raven Motel, United States, 1986; 02:13 repeating motif; first-person psychological/cooperative survival horror; 1–4 players; solo completion mandatory; target duration 40–50 minutes. Core loop: explore, inspect evidence/items, solve puzzle, open access/restore power, evade THE GUEST, rescue/recover, reveal ROOM 13, select one ending.

## Canonical act order
1. ACT I — THE ARRIVAL (0–8)
2. ACT II — THE REGISTER (8–17)
3. ACT III — THE POWER (17–27)
4. ACT IV — THE SECOND FLOOR (27–36)
5. ACT V — ROOM 13 (36–43)
6. ACT VI — NO VACANCY (43–50)

ACT II includes reception/register, office access and photo clue, rooms 01–06 and missing-guest evidence; its exit objective is restoring power. ACT III is the dedicated fuse/electrical restoration and first full pursuit. The office photo puzzle is distinct from the ROOM 13 four-photo chronology puzzle. The older Verse enum uses seven implementation phases and different labels; map it to these six narrative acts rather than treating it as a competing story structure.

## Canonical session flags
RegisterSolved; OfficeAccessGranted; OfficePhotoPuzzleSolved; UpperFloorAccessGranted; FuseAFound; FuseBFound; FuseCFound; PowerRestored; DiaryFound; ChildRoomClueFound; SecretPanelOpened; Room13Unlocked; Room13PhotoSequenceSolved; Room13RevealComplete; ServiceKeyObtained; CarKeysObtained; CoreEscapeReady; TruthChainComplete; ServiceRouteReady; NewGuestAccepted; EndingSelected.

TruthChainComplete must be computed from an explicitly approved evidence checklist. It must not be inferred solely from opening ROOM 13.

## Solo/co-op invariant
Every mandatory clue is readable without relying on audio; interactions are repeatable; quest state/items are team-shared and idempotent; disconnects cannot remove progress; simultaneous interactions cannot duplicate rewards; capture cannot interrupt protected mandatory interactions; solo capture has self-recovery; co-op New Guest requires team confirmation/shared outcome.

## Unresolved canon gaps
Exact register entries/code; office photo solution; fuse locations; truth evidence checklist; Car Keys/Screwdriver location; final generator/gate/vehicle interaction; THE TRUTH confrontation action/cost; THE NEW GUEST interaction text and co-op vote rule. These remain gaps, not invented facts.
